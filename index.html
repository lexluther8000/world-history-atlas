Earth has changed dramatically throughout its history. Continents have moved, civilizations have risen and fallen, and millions of years of geological and human history have shaped the world we know today.

One of the most famous chapters in Earth's geological history concerns Pangaea. This enormous supercontinent brought together much of the world's landmass before eventually breaking apart.

The breakup of Pangaea happened over millions of years. As tectonic plates moved, the supercontinent separated into smaller landmasses. These movements eventually helped produce the continents and oceans familiar to people today.

Human history is equally complex. Historians examine written records, archaeological evidence, oral traditions, artifacts, maps, and many other sources in an attempt to understand the past.

The study of how historians interpret and reconstruct the past is called historiography .
